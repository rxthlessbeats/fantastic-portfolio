## Introduction

This project builds on **Liu, Oosterlee, and Bohte**, who used artificial neural networks (ANNs) to price European options and infer **implied volatility** faster than iterative solvers. We replicate their experiments, then improve the pipeline with **Optuna** hyperparameter search, learning-rate decay tuning, and **TensorFlow model pruning** (node sparsity).

Compared with the original BS-ANN and IV-ANN setups, our enhancements target a **~10% accuracy gain** (lower MSE on train and test) and **~50% faster** implied-volatility inference—without relying on a full Heston implied-volatility surrogate. We also benchmark against **Random Forest** and **XGBoost** regressors on the same data splits.

## Problem & models

**Goal:** Learn a fast surrogate f<sub>θ</sub>(x) ≈ y where inputs are market parameters and outputs are either **scaled call prices** (Black–Scholes) or **implied volatility** σ.

**Asset dynamics:** Geometric Brownian motion underpins the Black–Scholes PDE for European calls. Implied volatility inverts the pricing map—traditionally solved with root-finding (Newton, Brent, bisection). We train ANNs to replace repeated PDE/solver calls at inference time.

**Data:** **Latin Hypercube Sampling** over wide parameter boxes (following the original paper), **1M** samples for Black–Scholes pricing; labels from closed-form BS. Implied-volatility data uses BS prices with gradient-friendly transforms (subtract intrinsic value, log time value) before ANN training. Splits: **90% train / 10% test**, multiple random seeds for stability.

## Methods

Pipeline (enhanced over Liu et al.):

1. Sample parameters (LHS) and compute labels (BS closed form or IV target).
2. Train/test split; train **MLP** with MSE loss.
3. **Optuna** (TPE sampler, ~100 trials) over depth, width, activation, optimizer, batch size, dropout, init, batch norm.
4. Tune **exponential learning-rate decay** after 1200 epochs at constant LR (decay rates from −0.1 to −0.01).
5. **Prune** IV-ANN with TensorFlow Model Optimization (sparsity 0.50 → 0.80, steps 2000–4000).
6. Deploy trained ANN in place of iterative solvers at inference.

## Results

**Black–Scholes (BS-ANN):** On the wide test set, root-averaged MSE implies pricing error on the order of **~0.007% of strike**; max deviation under **0.04%**. Training on a **wider** parameter box than the deployment range improved robustness. Our Optuna + decay setup reduced train/test MSE by roughly **10%** vs the original BS-ANN and beat **Random Forest** / **XGBoost** on the same features.

**Implied volatility (IV-ANN):** Input scaling (intrinsic subtraction + log time value) was critical—unscaled inputs degraded MSE sharply. Pruned IV-ANN kept accuracy while cutting compute.

**Solver comparison (20k European calls, GPU timing):** IV-ANN remains the fastest robust option; our pruned model reaches **~0.1 s** vs **~0.2 s** for the original IV-ANN on GPU (~**50%** speedup). Classical Brent is accurate but slower; Newton and secant trade robustness for speed.

## Key takeaways

- **Optuna** improved depth/width, optimizer, and batch settings beyond the fixed 4×400 MLP; tuned **LR decay** mattered as much as architecture (over-aggressive decay stalled MSE).
- **Input engineering** for IV (intrinsic removal + log time value) and **wide LHS training** were as important as network size.
- **Pruning** (50%→80% sparsity) delivered ~**50%** faster IV inference with little accuracy loss.
- ANN surrogates excel when batch GPU inference replaces thousands of root-finding iterations; tree models are competitive on price regression but not on end-to-end IV speed.

## Limitations & future work

**Limitations:** European calls under GBM/Black–Scholes only; replication-focused rather than production risk systems; Heston/COS paths noted in the paper but not the main ANN target here.

**Future work:** Extend Optuna + pruning to Heston surrogates; joint price–IV models; uncertainty calibration; deployment benchmarks on live volatility surfaces.
