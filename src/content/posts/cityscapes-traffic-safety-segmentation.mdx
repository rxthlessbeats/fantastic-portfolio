## Introduction

This project studies semantic segmentation for traffic safety in urban scenes using the [Cityscapes](https://www.cityscapes-dataset.com/) benchmark. Original labels are remapped into eight classes for training, with focus on **road**, **sidewalk**, **human**, and **vehicle** for safety analysis.

We implement and compare three models in **PyTorch**:

- **FCN-8s** — fully convolutional baseline
- **U-Net** — encoder–decoder with skip connections
- **DeepLabV3+** — context-aware model with atrous (dilated) convolutions

Models are trained under the same conditions and evaluated using **mean Intersection over Union (mIoU)** and **per-class IoU**.

## Problem & dataset

**Problem:** Predict which parts of a street scene are drivable, where pedestrians are, and where vehicles are—pixel by pixel—so downstream systems can reason about traffic safety.

**Dataset:** Cityscapes provides high-resolution street-view images (1024 × 2048) with fine semantic masks. We use the finely annotated training split (**3,475 images**, 50 cities), pairing RGB images (`leftImg8bit`) with labels (`gtFine`).

**Labels:** Original Cityscapes categories are merged into **8 classes** (road, sidewalk, human, vehicle, traffic objects, nature, construction, background) to simplify training while keeping safety-relevant structure.

## Methods

All three architectures share the same training protocol (optimizer, learning rate schedule, input resolution policy, and epoch budget) so differences reflect model design rather than tuning imbalance.

## Key takeaways

- **FCN-8s** is a solid baseline, but downsampling limits boundary precision.
- **U-Net** recovers finer detail via skip connections and improves over FCN-8s.
- **DeepLabV3+** achieves the best validation accuracy and **mIoU**, combining ASPP multi-scale context with a strong decoder—best suited to urban scenes with both large regions and small objects.
- L2 regularization, learning rate decay, and early stopping kept overfitting in check; train–val gaps stayed relatively stable (see training curves below).
- **Human** and **traffic object** classes remain the hardest due to class imbalance and small, thin structures.

## Limitations & future work

**Limitations:** Higher resolution may help rare classes but costs more time and memory; aggressive rebalancing and augmentation did not consistently raise mIoU; evaluation used only the **8-class** remap (results may differ for full 19-class Cityscapes).

**Future work:** Full-resolution training, transformer-based segmenters, lighter augmentation to avoid distribution shift, and targeted strategies for **human** and **traffic object** classes.
